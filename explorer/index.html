cool chess moves you need to know

castling

Castling is a move in chess where if the king and rook have no pieces in between the can move across from eachother.

If the king and rook haven't moved they can castle, if one of them did move, you cant castle.

you can castle with both either rook.

en passant

this is a move where if whites pawn is on the the 5th rank and if black pushes their pawn by 2 squares and their pawn is side by side with your pawn then you can move diagonally towards the black pawn and capture the black pawn.

you can do the same with black if blacks pawn is on the 4th rank and white pushes their pawn by 2 squares black en passant.

note:if you dont do en passant right after the opponent moves the pawn en passant cant be possible.

under promotion

It might feel counterintuitive to not promote to a queen but its a very situational move.

In some scenarios if you like in a an endgame, if your not careful you could accidentally stalemate.

Note: you should almost always promote to a queen but make sure that the enemy king is not in a stalemating position

taking the a free file with the rook

This is a really powerful move in chess, it requires a rook and if you put the rook in an empty file that rook will have full mobility to travel across the board, and it will be very dangerous for the opponent to put a piece of theirs on that file.

note this move is usually done in the endgame

forks

A fork is a tactical move where one piece attacks two or more opponent pieces at the same time.

you can fork with a knight, bishop, queen, and even a pawn(with protection).

note: forks are best done with a knight because only the knight can move in an L-shape

a more deadly fork is with a check, if you check the enemy king with your fork, you are winning material no matter what.

pin

a pin is a move where one piece attacks an enemy piece where that enemy piece cannot move without exposing a more valuable piece behind it.

there is something called an absolute pin that happens when the king is behind a pinned piece and the pinned piece is not allowed to move at all.

note: only bishops, rooks, and queens can create pins.

skewer

a skewer is basically the opposite of an absolute pin, where the king is infront and another piece and you give a check while the piece is behind the king and its really dangerous.

note: a skewer is only possible with a bishop, rook, or queen.
